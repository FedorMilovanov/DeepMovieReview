# DeepMovieReview — Experience Blueprint

> Status: v0.1 concept blueprint  
> Goal: translate the visual constitution and domain model into concrete surfaces.

## 1. Homepage — proposed sequence

This is a storyboard, not final copy.

### 01 — Black / Thesis

Visual:

- near-total black;
- small pointer/focus mark;
- tiny grain;
- no card grid.

Purpose: establish seriousness and intrigue.

Possible thesis structure:

- stories make moral claims;
- we examine acts, motives, consequences and what the film asks us to believe.

Motion: minimal type reveal.

Mobile: same idea, shorter vertical dwell.

### 02 — The Frame

Visual:

- 2.39:1 cinematic frame appears;
- one master artwork;
- frame corners / tiny timecode;
- slight depth response.

Interaction: Moral Lens introduces itself without tutorial text overload.

### 03 — Dissection Introduction

The frame begins to separate into analytical layers.

Reveal only three concepts first:

- ACT;
- MOTIVE;
- CONSEQUENCE.

Do not expose the whole methodology yet.

### 04 — Why Sin Counting Alone Is Not Enough

Editorial section with clear principles:

- depiction is not endorsement;
- explanation is not justification;
- responsibility depends on knowledge, intention and freedom;
- the film's own stance matters.

This section should be very readable, with little GPU motion.

### 05 — Featured Film

One film as a cinematic case study.

- large visual;
- short verdict;
- 2–4 high-level dimensions;
- enter analysis.

Transition: selected media expands into film hero.

### 06 — Moral Timeline Preview

A short interactive fragment of timeline communicates the product better than marketing prose.

### 07 — Decision Chamber Preview

One real dilemma with “what the character knew” vs later viewer knowledge.

No community distribution in MVP; later phase can add vote-first/crowd-second.

### 08 — Moral Core

Show how a film's structured analysis generates a fingerprint.

Do not imply the sculpture itself is mystical or authoritative; it is a visualization of explicit dimensions.

### 09 — Numbers / Database Scale

Only after enough corpus exists.

Potential future counts:

- films analyzed;
- scenes indexed;
- moral events;
- dilemmas;
- principles.

Avoid fake launch counters.

### 10 — Moral Atlas

Preview cross-film discovery by theme and structure.

### 11 — Scripture / Norm

Warm editorial section explaining that description and popularity do not define the biblical norm.

### 12 — Methodology

Transparent link to methodology and version.

### 13 — Catalog Entry

Fast search and browse, not only immersive Explore mode.

### 14 — Footer

Quiet, functional, accessible.

## 2. Film page — proposed 30-section library

Not every film uses every section. This is a component library / content grammar.

### 01. Film Hero

- title;
- year/runtime;
- master artwork;
- minimal editorial marker;
- spoiler-safe.

### 02. Verdict Snapshot

Only essential high-level judgment.

### 03. Viewer Score Hook (future)

Separate user film-quality score. Hidden until community phase.

### 04. Spoiler Control

User selects spoiler depth.

### 05. Synopsis

Quiet text.

### 06. Why This Film Matters

The analytical thesis in 2–4 paragraphs.

### 07. What the Film Appears to Say

Separate theme/narrative claim from content inventory.

### 08. Moral Core Overview

A film fingerprint with plain-language explanation.

### 09. Moral Timeline Intro

Introduce visual vocabulary.

### 10. Full Moral Timeline

Interactive sequence of meaningful events.

### 11. Event Summary

Counts by type/severity, secondary to interpretation.

### 12. Character Map

Key characters and relationships.

### 13. Character Moral Arc

Fall, resistance, self-deception, repentance, restoration, etc.

### 14. Psychological X-Ray

Motivations and rationalizations.

### 15. Scene Autopsy A

Deep evidence-based breakdown.

### 16. Scene Autopsy B

Optional second pattern where needed.

### 17. Act / Motive Distinction

Editorial explanation of a central case.

### 18. Knowledge / Freedom / Coercion

Responsibility analysis.

### 19. Difficult Decision A

Decision Chamber.

### 20. Difficult Decision B

Optional comparison.

### 21. Character Perspective vs Viewer Perspective

Knowledge Fog interaction.

### 22. Depiction vs Endorsement

Evidence for the film's stance.

### 23. Empathy Pressure

How filmmaking encourages identification, sympathy or admiration.

### 24. Consequences

Who bears the cost and whether the film shows it.

### 25. Repentance / Restitution

What changes, if anything.

### 26. Redemptive Movement

Broader direction of the story.

### 27. Biblical Principles

Normative analysis with careful citations.

### 28. Counterevidence / Ambiguity

Strongest evidence against our reading; confidence state.

### 29. Final Verdict

Calm, nearly motionless synthesis.

### 30. Related Moral Parallels

Similar dilemmas/themes in other films.

### 31. Community Reveal (future)

Viewer distributions appear only after editorial conclusion.

### 32. Counterarguments (future)

Strongest community arguments.

### 33. Reception Over Time (future)

Historical community change.

### 34. Sources / Method Version

Transparent methodology and revision history.

## 3. Scene page / deep link

A scene should be shareable independently when useful.

Components:

- film context;
- timestamp / edition;
- spoiler warning;
- short scene summary;
- Moral Lens visual;
- moral events;
- claims + evidence;
- decision if applicable;
- biblical principles;
- related scenes;
- future community responses.

## 4. Character page

Potential long-term structure:

- character overview;
- timeline of decisions;
- dominant motives;
- moral events;
- relationship graph;
- psychological patterns;
- responsibility changes over story;
- consequences;
- repentance / restoration;
- related characters across cinema.

Avoid gamified “evil score.”

## 5. Moral topic page

Example: `Revenge`.

Sections:

- definition / biblical principle;
- distinctions (justice vs revenge, anger vs vengeance, etc.);
- films containing the topic;
- strongest examples;
- contrasting narrative stances;
- common rationalizations;
- related dilemmas;
- community disagreement (later).

## 6. Dilemma page

Example: `Lying to protect an innocent person`.

Sections:

- precise moral question;
- principle framework;
- recurring variables;
- film cases;
- comparison matrix;
- character knowledge;
- coercion;
- intended vs actual consequences;
- editorial analyses;
- audience decisions (later).

This can become one of the platform's most distinctive research surfaces.

## 7. Compare mode

Possible comparisons:

- film vs film;
- character vs character;
- decision vs decision;
- Moral Core vs Moral Core;
- editorial vs audience;
- two editions of one film.

Use synchronized interaction where it improves understanding.

## 10. User participation surfaces (future)

### Rate This Film

Fast quality/enjoyment rating.

### Moral Perception

Categorical film-stance response.

### Judge the Scene

Deep structured interaction.

### Community Reveal

Post-analysis aggregate reveal.

### Moral Mirror

Personal descriptive comparison, never spiritual scoring.

## 11. Responsive principles

Mobile is not “desktop with WebGL removed.”

Recompose:

- fewer simultaneous layers;
- vertical evidence relationships;
- tap/hold instead of hover;
- explicit controls around 3D;
- static/lightweight Core if needed;
- no tiny forensic labels;
- timeline may switch from horizontal cinematic track to vertical sequence;
- Decision Chamber becomes stacked but preserves known/unknown distinction.

## 12. Loading strategy by section

Each deep section should be independently lazy-loadable.

Example:

- hero + verdict load first;
- timeline manifest next;
- detailed scene assets only shortly before viewport;
- Decision Chamber assets on demand;
- community bundle only if opened;
- compare tools only on compare route.

## 13. Experience rule

At any scroll position, a user should be able to answer:

1. What am I looking at?
2. Why does this visual behavior exist?
3. What claim or evidence does it communicate?
4. Can I continue without interacting with the GPU effect?

If not, simplify the section.
