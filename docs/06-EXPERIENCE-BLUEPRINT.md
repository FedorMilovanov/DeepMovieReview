# DeepMovieReview — Experience Blueprint

> Status: v0.2 concept blueprint  
> Goal: translate the visual constitution and expanded domain model into concrete surfaces.

## 1. Homepage

The authoritative homepage storyboard now lives in:

[`11-HOMEPAGE-ARCHITECTURE.md`](11-HOMEPAGE-ARCHITECTURE.md)

Its central correction is that the homepage must demonstrate whole-film analysis before the explicit biblical/moral verdict.

Primary sequence:

```text
FILM
→ STORY
→ PEOPLE
→ RELATIONSHIPS
→ IDEAS / MEANING
→ WHAT THE FILM TEACHES / NORMALIZES
→ CRAFT / SYMPATHY
→ MORAL FORENSICS
→ BIBLICAL LENS
→ SYNTHESIS / DISCOVERY
```

Key homepage signature modules:

- Living Film Frame;
- Six Lenses (`Story / People / Relationships / Ideas / Moral World / Craft`);
- Relationship Observatory;
- Family / Parents / Youth preview;
- `What is the Film Saying?`;
- Message Field;
- Narrative Permission Map;
- Form Shapes Sympathy;
- Scene Autopsy;
- Decision Chamber / Knowledge Fog;
- Biblical Lens;
- Featured Analysis;
- Explore by question, not only genre.

## 2. Film page — proposed module library

Not every film uses every module. This is a content grammar/component library.

### A. Entry / spoiler-safe orientation

#### 01. Film Hero / Living Frame

- title;
- year/runtime;
- key credits;
- master editorial/official artwork;
- minimal editorial marker;
- spoiler-safe.

#### 02. Story at a Glance

- premise;
- central conflict;
- spoiler-safe story question.

#### 03. Analysis Snapshot

Only essential high-level findings.

Do not front-load a wall of scores.

#### 04. Viewer Score Hook (future)

Separate user film-quality/enjoyment score. Hidden until community phase.

#### 05. Spoiler Control

User selects spoiler depth. Spoiler state applies to structured modules, not only text blur.

### B. Story / plot

#### 06. Synopsis

Quiet editorial text. Can switch between safe/extended/full versions.

#### 07. Story Structure

Visualized plot/causal structure:

- setup;
- conflict;
- major turns;
- crisis/climax;
- resolution.

Do not force one screenplay template onto every film.

#### 08. Why the Story Works / Fails

Storytelling evaluation:

- causal coherence;
- setup/payoff;
- pacing;
- internal rules;
- ending coherence.

#### 09. Plot Trace

A focused causal chain for one key story thread.

Uses `TRACE`, not a generic flowchart.

### C. Characters / psychology

#### 10. Character Constellation

Key cast relationships and narrative weight.

#### 11. Character Portrait

For a central character:

- desire;
- fear;
- loyalties;
- contradiction;
- self-image;
- role in the film's argument.

#### 12. Character Arc

Track transformation, hardening, fall, growth, repentance, sacrifice, restoration or deliberate stasis.

#### 13. Psychological X-Ray

Motivation, self-deception, rationalization, fear, pride, conformity, shame/guilt and pressure.

Explanation remains separate from justification.

#### 14. Image of the Person

What image of human nature/personhood does the film construct?

This is interpretive prose/claims, not a score by default.

### D. Relationships / social models

#### 15. Relationship Observatory

Signature module.

A relationship is shown across meaningful scenes rather than as a static chart.

Possible dimensions:

- trust;
- honesty;
- reciprocity;
- responsibility;
- power;
- boundaries;
- sacrifice;
- conflict;
- repair.

#### 16. Relationship Trace

Timeline of relational turning points.

#### 17. Marriage / Romance

When central:

- what the film says love is;
- commitment/fidelity;
- desire vs self-giving;
- manipulation/control;
- conflict/repair;
- ending model.

#### 18. Parent & Child

When central:

- presence;
- responsibility;
- authority;
- warmth/protection;
- modeling;
- hypocrisy;
- discipline/boundaries;
- repair;
- child response/maturation.

#### 19. Family System

Who carries responsibility? What patterns repeat? Is authority absent, wise, abusive, indulgent or hypocritical?

#### 20. Youth & Formation

Analyze:

- peer pressure;
- rebellion vs healthy autonomy;
- parent/teacher relation;
- risk behavior;
- sexuality/romance;
- work/school;
- consequences;
- adult role models;
- what the film calls maturity.

This is not an age-appropriateness score.

#### 21. Friendship / Loyalty

Especially useful for loyalty vs complicity, betrayal, sacrifice and truth-telling.

#### 22. Authority / Institutions

Parents, teachers, police, courts, church leaders, employers, government, military, etc. where relevant.

### E. Ideas / meaning / worldview

#### 23. Themes

Recurring subjects/tensions.

#### 24. Narrative Questions

The serious questions the film explores.

#### 25. What the Film Appears to Say

Evidence-backed narrative claims with confidence.

#### 26. Counterclaim / Ambiguity

Strongest evidence complicating our interpretation.

#### 27. Worldview Claims

Possible domains:

- human nature;
- moral order;
- freedom/responsibility;
- happiness/flourishing;
- family/relationships;
- justice/power;
- meaning/purpose;
- spiritual/transcendent claims.

Avoid one `worldview score`.

#### 28. Discussion Questions

Questions the film makes worth discussing, including cases where the film's answer is weak or wrong.

#### 29. Discussion Value

Optional synthesis: how much serious discussion the film can sustain. High value does not imply moral correctness.

### F. What the film teaches / permits

#### 30. What the Film Teaches by Example

Separate channels:

- explicit lesson;
- role model;
- cautionary model;
- reward;
- cost;
- normalization;
- humor;
- ridicule;
- ending resolution;
- unchallenged assumption.

#### 31. Role Models / Anti-Models

A protagonist is not automatically a role model.

Candidate classifications:

- aspirational;
- cautionary;
- mixed;
- charismatic-but-destructive;
- responsible adult;
- corrupt mentor;
- comic anti-model.

#### 32. Narrative Permission Map

Candidate states:

`CONDEMNED / COSTLY / QUESTIONED / UNCHALLENGED / NORMALIZED / REWARDED / CELEBRATED / AMBIGUOUS`

Critical distinction:

`UNCHALLENGED ≠ ENDORSED`.

#### 33. Ending Weight

How the ending confirms, reverses, complicates or evades earlier signals.

### G. Craft shapes meaning

#### 34. Craft & Meaning

Direction, camera, editing, music, performance, production design and genre as evidence.

#### 35. Form Shapes Sympathy

How filmmaking asks us to identify, pity, admire, fear or reject.

#### 36. Empathy Pressure

Understanding/feeling with a character is not endorsement.

#### 37. Imitation Pressure

How strongly a lifestyle/behavior is made aspirational, glamorous, socially rewarded or identity-conferring.

Especially relevant to youth-facing analyses.

### H. Moral forensics

#### 38. Moral Core Overview

Structured synthesis visualization with plain-language explanation.

#### 39. Moral Timeline

Sequence of significant acts, virtues, failures, decisions, consequences and restoration.

#### 40. Moral Event Summary

Counts/types remain secondary to interpretation.

#### 41. Scene Autopsy A

Evidence-based breakdown:

- act;
- motive;
- knowledge;
- pressure;
- consequence;
- film framing.

#### 42. Scene Autopsy B

Optional second major pattern.

#### 43. Act / Motive / Intention

Deep distinction for a central case.

#### 44. Knowledge / Freedom / Coercion

Responsibility analysis.

#### 45. Difficult Decision / Decision Chamber

Duties, options, pressure and foreseeable consequences.

#### 46. Character Perspective vs Viewer Perspective

Knowledge Fog interaction.

#### 47. Depiction vs Endorsement

Evidence for narrative stance.

#### 48. Consequences

Who bears the cost? Does the film show it, displace it or erase it?

#### 49. Repentance / Restitution

Recognition, remorse, confession, repair and restitution where relevant.

#### 50. Redemptive Movement

Broader movement toward or away from truth, justice, mercy, sacrificial love, reconciliation and restoration.

### I. Biblical / normative analysis

#### 51. Biblical Principles

Normative analysis with careful Scripture/theological evidence.

Distinguish:

- direct command/prohibition;
- broader moral principle;
- wisdom judgment;
- disputed application;
- prudential judgment.

#### 52. Relationship Norms

Where needed, compare observed patterns to specific biblical principles such as truthfulness, fidelity, sacrificial love, parental responsibility, justice, boundaries, reconciliation and restitution.

Avoid abstract `ideal relationship score`.

#### 53. Biblical Evaluation of Film Claims

Assess not only individual acts, but the film's claims about:

- personhood;
- love;
- family;
- maturity;
- authority;
- justice;
- meaning;
- hope;
- God/spiritual reality where relevant.

#### 54. Counterevidence / Interpretive Uncertainty

A serious conclusion should show the strongest evidence against itself.

#### 55. Final Synthesis / Verdict

Calm, almost motionless.

Synthesize:

- film as art;
- story;
- characters;
- relationships;
- themes/messages;
- moral world;
- biblical judgment.

Do not end with one reductive master number.

### J. Discovery / community

#### 56. Related Narrative Parallels

Similar themes/questions across films.

#### 57. Related Relationship Parallels

Comparable marriage, parent-child, friendship, youth or authority patterns.

#### 58. Related Moral Parallels

Comparable dilemmas/events.

#### 59. Community Reveal (future)

Viewer distributions appear separately from editorial conclusion.

#### 60. Counterarguments (future)

Strongest community arguments, ranked for reasoning/helpfulness rather than mere agreement.

#### 61. Reception Over Time (future)

Historical community change.

#### 62. Sources / Method Version

Transparent methodology, sources and revision history.

## 3. Scene page / deep link

A scene should be independently shareable when useful.

Components can include:

- film/edition context;
- timestamp;
- spoiler warning;
- scene summary;
- image / Living Frame;
- characters/relationships present;
- plot function;
- craft observations;
- theme/message evidence;
- moral events;
- claims + evidence;
- decision if applicable;
- biblical principles;
- related scenes;
- future community responses.

## 4. Character page

Potential long-term structure:

- character overview;
- desire/fear/contradiction;
- story arc;
- relationship graph;
- key decisions;
- psychological patterns;
- moral events;
- role-model/anti-model analysis;
- what the film asks us to feel about the character;
- consequences;
- repentance/restoration;
- related character patterns across cinema.

Avoid gamified `evil score`.

## 5. Relationship page / deep link

Potential later surface:

- relationship participants/type;
- initial state;
- Relationship Trace;
- trust/honesty/power/responsibility patterns;
- conflict and repair;
- key scenes;
- film framing;
- normative principles where applicable;
- comparable relationships in other films.

## 6. Theme / topic page

Example: `Fatherhood`, `Revenge`, `Truth`, `Marriage`.

Sections:

- definition/question space;
- narrative claims across films;
- contrasting examples;
- related relationships/characters;
- film-form patterns;
- biblical framework where normative;
- related dilemmas;
- community disagreement later.

## 7. Dilemma page

Example: `Lying to protect an innocent person`.

Sections:

- precise moral question;
- principle framework;
- recurring variables;
- film cases;
- comparison matrix;
- character knowledge;
- coercion;
- intended vs actual consequences;
- editorial analyses;
- audience decisions later.

## 8. Compare mode

Possible comparisons:

- film vs film;
- character vs character;
- relationship vs relationship;
- narrative claim vs narrative claim;
- decision vs decision;
- Moral Core vs Moral Core;
- editorial vs audience;
- two editions of one film.

Use synchronized interaction only where it improves understanding.

## 11. User participation surfaces (future)

### Rate This Film

Fast quality/enjoyment rating.

### Interpretation / Moral Perception

Categorical audience perception, never biblical authority.

### Judge the Scene

Structured decision/moral interaction.

### Community Reveal

Post-response aggregate reveal.

### Moral Mirror

Personal descriptive comparison, never spiritual scoring.

## 12. Responsive principles

Mobile is not `desktop with WebGL removed`.

Recompose:

- fewer simultaneous layers;
- vertical plot/relationship traces;
- tap/hold instead of hover;
- explicit controls around 3D;
- static/lightweight Core if needed;
- no tiny forensic labels;
- horizontal timelines may become vertical sequences;
- Decision Chamber becomes stacked but preserves known/unknown distinction;
- Relationship Observatory uses one active relationship at a time.

## 13. Loading strategy

Each deep section should be independently lazy-loadable.

Example:

- hero + story/analysis snapshot first;
- character/relationship text next;
- heavy depth assets shortly before viewport;
- detailed timeline/scene assets on approach;
- Decision Chamber assets on demand;
- community bundle only if opened;
- compare tools only on compare route.

## 14. Experience rule

At any scroll position, a user should be able to answer:

1. What am I looking at?
2. Is this story, character, relationship, theme, craft, moral or biblical analysis?
3. Why does this visual behavior exist?
4. What claim or evidence does it communicate?
5. Can I continue without interacting with the GPU effect?

If not, simplify the section.
