# DeepMovieReview — Rating & Analysis Methodology

> Status: v0.2 foundation  
> This is a methodological framework, not final theology or final numerical calibration.

## 1. First principle: no single master score

A film can be artistically excellent and morally dark. It can depict extreme evil while strongly condemning it. It can present psychologically credible but morally corrupt characters. It can portray a dysfunctional family without prescribing dysfunction as ideal. It can raise profound questions while giving confused answers.

Therefore DeepMovieReview must keep independent analytical domains.

### Core domains

- **Film Craft / Cinematic Quality**
- **Storytelling / Narrative Coherence**
- **Character Writing / Psychological Realism**
- **Relationships / Social Models**
- **Themes / Meaning / Worldview**
- **Teaching / Narrative Formation Signals**
- **Moral Content / Severity**
- **Narrative Moral Stance**
- **Decision Complexity**
- **Repentance / Redemptive Movement**
- **Interpretive Confidence**

A compact UI may summarize them, but storage and reasoning remain separate.

## 2. Three interpretive axioms

### Depiction ≠ Endorsement

A film may depict evil while condemning it. Presence, quantity or graphic intensity cannot be used as a proxy for the film's moral claim.

### Explanation ≠ Justification

Psychological/social explanation does not automatically reduce moral culpability.

### Representation ≠ Prescription

A film can represent a relationship, family pattern, youth culture, social practice or worldview without presenting it as an ideal.

For representation claims, examine:

- frequency;
- framing;
- consequences;
- reward/cost;
- admiration/ridicule;
- counterexamples;
- ending resolution;
- genre/satire;
- confidence.

## 3. Analytical order

The preferred editorial order is:

1. understand the story;
2. understand the characters;
3. understand relationships and social roles;
4. identify themes/questions;
5. identify narrative claims/messages;
6. examine how film form creates sympathy and meaning;
7. analyze morally significant actions/decisions;
8. determine narrative stance and permission patterns;
9. connect normative judgments to biblical principles;
10. synthesize with counterevidence/confidence.

This order prevents premature moral labeling from replacing film analysis.

## 4. Story / plot analysis

### Storytelling dimensions

Candidate dimensions:

- causal coherence;
- character motivation continuity;
- setup/payoff;
- pacing;
- structural clarity;
- internal rules;
- ending coherence;
- integration of subplots;
- thematic integration.

Do not force every film into one screenplay formula.

### Synopsis discipline

Keep spoiler-safe premise, extended synopsis and full structural synopsis separate.

A synopsis is descriptive, not a verdict.

## 5. Character analysis

For central characters ask:

- What does the character want?
- What does the character fear?
- What loyalties shape decisions?
- What contradiction/self-deception exists?
- What changes?
- What remains unchanged?
- Is the arc psychologically credible?
- Is the character presented as aspirational, cautionary, mixed or merely focal?
- How strongly does the film invite identification?

### Character dimensions

Possible public/internal dimensions:

- character depth;
- psychological realism;
- motivational coherence;
- arc credibility;
- internal consistency.

Avoid clinical diagnosis unless appropriately supported.

## 6. Relationship analysis

Relationships are evaluated descriptively first, normatively second.

### Descriptive dimensions/tags

- trust;
- honesty;
- communication;
- reciprocity;
- fidelity/commitment where applicable;
- responsibility;
- affection;
- sacrifice;
- respect;
- power asymmetry;
- boundaries;
- manipulation;
- control;
- enabling;
- conflict;
- forgiveness;
- repair;
- restitution;
- stability.

### Normative questions

- Are duties fulfilled or neglected?
- Is love ordered toward the other person's good or toward possession/control?
- Does loyalty become complicity?
- Does forgiveness erase justice or responsibility?
- Is authority used for service/protection or domination?
- Are boundaries violated?
- Is reconciliation truthful and substantive?

Do not create a simplistic `relationship morality score`.

## 7. Parent / child and family analysis

When central to a film, analyze at least:

### Parent/caregiver

- presence;
- attentiveness;
- warmth;
- protection;
- provision;
- consistency;
- instruction;
- discipline/boundaries;
- modeling;
- hypocrisy;
- sacrifice;
- negligence;
- indulgence;
- abuse/manipulation;
- enabling;
- repair.

### Child/adolescent

- honesty;
- respect/contempt;
- healthy autonomy vs irresponsible rebellion;
- responsibility;
- peer pressure;
- school/work behavior;
- risk-taking;
- sexuality/romance where relevant;
- substances where relevant;
- teachability;
- maturation;
- consequences.

### Family-system questions

- Who actually bears responsibility?
- Are adults present/competent?
- Are children forced into adult roles?
- Is authority wise, arbitrary, abusive, absent, indulgent or caricatured?
- What patterns repeat across generations?
- Does the family repair conflict or merely suppress it?

## 8. Youth / formation analysis

Do not reduce youth-facing analysis to age appropriateness.

Ask:

- What behavior is presented as normal adolescence?
- What counts as maturity within the film?
- Are parents/teachers made uniformly foolish merely to validate rebellion?
- Is peer approval treated as decisive?
- Are sex, alcohol, drugs, dishonesty or risk framed as rites of passage?
- Are consequences visible?
- Which adult models are present?
- What picture of adulthood is offered?

Possible public surface: `YOUTH & FORMATION`.

## 9. Themes, narrative questions and meaning

### Theme

Recurring subject/tension.

### Narrative Question

A question substantially explored by the story.

### Narrative Claim

The film's apparent answer, inferred from evidence.

Each claim should support:

- evidence;
- counterevidence;
- ending weight;
- confidence;
- spoiler level.

Avoid pretending creator intent is known unless sourced.

## 10. Worldview analysis

Do not use one opaque worldview score.

Potential claim families:

- human nature;
- moral order;
- freedom/responsibility;
- meaning/purpose;
- happiness/flourishing;
- love/relationships;
- family/generations;
- authority/power;
- justice;
- spiritual/transcendent claims.

A film can be truthful in one domain and confused in another.

## 11. What the film teaches: teaching-signal model

Films shape viewers through more than explicit speeches.

Possible channels:

1. **Explicit lesson** — dialogue/narration/resolution states a claim.
2. **Role model** — a character is presented as admirable/aspirational.
3. **Cautionary model** — a pattern is shown as destructive.
4. **Reward** — behavior leads to desired outcome/status/acceptance.
5. **Cost/punishment** — behavior brings meaningful consequence.
6. **Normalization** — repeated behavior is treated as ordinary and socially frictionless.
7. **Romanticization/aestheticization** — filmmaking makes a pattern attractive.
8. **Humor/comic permission** — behavior is insulated from seriousness through comedy.
9. **Ridicule** — a belief/person is made contemptible through framing.
10. **Unchallenged assumption** — behavior/claim remains largely unexamined.
11. **Ending resolution** — final state confirms, reverses or complicates earlier signals.

### Critical caution

`UNCHALLENGED ≠ ENDORSED`.

But a repeated unchallenged pattern combined with admiration, reward and absence of victims/consequences can still be a meaningful formation signal.

## 12. Narrative Permission

A broader categorical model for how the film's world treats a behavior/value/pattern.

Candidate states:

- `CONDEMNED`
- `COSTLY`
- `QUESTIONED`
- `UNCHALLENGED`
- `NORMALIZED`
- `REWARDED`
- `CELEBRATED`
- `AMBIGUOUS`

Modifiers:

- romanticized;
- aestheticized;
- comic;
- tragic;
- satirical;
- excused;
- grieved;
- culturally assumed.

Narrative Permission is not a final biblical verdict. It describes the practical moral atmosphere of the story.

## 13. Role models and anti-models

Do not equate protagonist with role model.

Candidate classifications:

- aspirational;
- cautionary;
- mixed;
- charismatic-but-destructive;
- comic anti-model;
- mentor;
- corrupt mentor;
- responsible adult;
- absent/ineffective adult;
- peer leader.

Questions:

- What traits does the film make admirable?
- Are flaws recognized or part of the charisma?
- Is success/reward attached to harmful behavior?
- Is there a countermodel?
- Might adolescents read the character differently from mature viewers?

## 14. Film form and reception

Craft is evidence, not merely a quality score.

Evidence for meaning/stance can include:

- camera proximity/POV;
- lighting/glamour;
- blocking;
- music;
- slow motion;
- editing/juxtaposition;
- reaction shots;
- performance;
- humor;
- genre conventions;
- production design;
- visual motifs.

### Empathy Pressure

How strongly the filmmaking invites understanding/feeling with a character.

Empathy is not endorsement.

### Imitation Pressure

How strongly the filmmaking makes behavior/lifestyle look desirable, glamorous, socially rewarded or identity-conferring.

Useful evidence:

- aspirational imagery;
- charisma;
- reward/success;
- peer approval;
- music;
- romantic framing;
- comic framing;
- erased victims/consequences;
- repeatable/copyable behavior.

## 15. Moral-event analysis unit

For each significant moral event ask:

1. **ACT** — What was done or omitted?
2. **OBJECT / TARGET** — Toward whom or what?
3. **MOTIVE** — Why did the character act?
4. **INTENTION** — What outcome was intended?
5. **KNOWLEDGE** — What was known at the time?
6. **FREEDOM** — How free was the choice?
7. **PRESSURE / COERCION** — What constrained the character?
8. **FORESEEABILITY** — What consequences were reasonably foreseeable?
9. **CONSEQUENCE** — What actually followed?
10. **CULPABILITY** — How responsible is the character?
11. **REPENTANCE / RESTITUTION** — Is wrongdoing recognized/addressed?
12. **NARRATIVE STANCE** — How does the film frame the event?
13. **CONFIDENCE** — How certain is our interpretation?

## 16. Depiction vs endorsement model

Evidence for narrative moral stance may include:

- consequences;
- character arc;
- framing/POV;
- music/aestheticization;
- rewarded/punished behavior;
- explicit dialogue;
- irony/satire;
- ending resolution;
- contrast characters;
- recurring motifs;
- what the film asks the viewer to admire, pity, fear or reject.

Suggested event/topic scale:

`CONDEMNS → QUESTIONS → AMBIVALENT → NORMALIZES → CELEBRATES`

Modifiers can coexist:

- romanticizes;
- aestheticizes;
- trivializes;
- excuses;
- grieves;
- satirizes;
- exposes;
- rewards;
- punishes.

## 17. Explanation vs justification model

Store psychological explanation separately from moral responsibility.

### Psychological explanation

- fear;
- shame;
- pride;
- attachment;
- trauma context where actually supported;
- self-preservation;
- conformity;
- revenge;
- anger;
- rationalization;
- identity/social pressure.

### Moral responsibility

- knowledge;
- freedom;
- coercion;
- intention;
- foreseeable harm;
- alternatives;
- duty.

A high-explanation signal does not automatically lower culpability.

## 18. Candidate editorial facets

These are candidates; final rubric must be calibrated on real films.

### Film / craft

- cinematic quality;
- direction;
- screenplay;
- acting;
- cinematography;
- editing;
- sound/music;
- production design;
- narrative coherence.

### Story / meaning

- storytelling;
- thematic depth;
- thematic coherence;
- worldview/message clarity;
- discussion value.

### Character / psychology

- character depth;
- psychological realism;
- motivational coherence;
- arc credibility.

### Relationship/social portrayal

Only when central:

- relational depth/realism;
- family/parent-child portrayal;
- marriage/romance portrayal;
- youth/formation portrayal;
- friendship/loyalty;
- authority/responsibility.

### Moral/biblical

- moral clarity;
- depicted evil severity;
- romanticization/normalization pressure;
- decision complexity;
- consequence visibility;
- repentance/restitution;
- redemptive movement.

Do not over-fragment the public UI. Internal structure can be much deeper than the surface.

## 19. Discussion Value

Candidate cross-domain dimension:

> How much substantial, evidence-based conversation can the film support?

Potential contributors:

- genuine dilemmas;
- thematic depth;
- relational complexity;
- worldview tension;
- psychological realism;
- meaningful ambiguity;
- strong counterevidence.

High discussion value does not mean correct moral answers.

## 20. Numerical vs categorical dimensions

Not every concept benefits from numbers.

### Numeric internally / optionally public

- film craft;
- storytelling;
- psychological realism;
- moral complexity;
- severity;
- confidence;
- audience polarization.

### Categorical preferred

- film stance toward an act;
- Narrative Permission;
- decision verdict;
- repentance state;
- pressure type;
- moral category;
- spoiler level;
- many relationship observations.

Public precision must never exceed methodological precision.

## 21. Confidence and counterevidence

Every interpretive claim can have confidence:

- `HIGH`
- `MEDIUM`
- `LOW`

Confidence depends on evidence clarity, not editor intensity.

Claims about themes/messages/relationships should include meaningful counterevidence when it exists.

Low-confidence claims must remain visibly tentative.

## 22. Biblical principle layer

The biblical layer must not be reduced to attaching a verse to a score.

Preferred chain:

`OBSERVATION / EVENT / RELATIONSHIP PATTERN → PRINCIPLE → SCRIPTURE → EXPLANATION → APPLICATION → VERDICT`

Distinguish:

- direct biblical prohibition/command;
- broader biblical moral principle;
- wisdom judgment;
- disputed application;
- prudential judgment.

The site must be willing to say that an application is debated or low-confidence.

## 23. Decision analysis rubric

For a difficult choice evaluate:

- duties in conflict;
- goods at stake;
- forbidden means;
- uncertainty;
- character knowledge;
- alternatives;
- time pressure;
- threat/coercion;
- foreseeable harm;
- intention;
- actual outcome;
- whether later consequences were knowable at decision time.

Required UI distinction:

**What the character knew then** vs **what the viewer knows later**.

## 24. Audience film rating

Future viewer enjoyment/quality score:

- separate from biblical/editorial judgment;
- likely 0.0–10.0 display;
- one current rating per user per film;
- rating history preserved;
- pre-release rating abuse prevented.

### Aggregate

Never use plain arithmetic mean as the sole public rating.

Use at least:

- prior/shrinkage for sparse titles;
- anomaly resistance;
- one effective vote per account;
- anti-brigading signals;
- confidence/maturity state;
- raw aggregates preserved.

Baseline example:

`weighted = (n / (n + m)) * R + (m / (n + m)) * C`

where `R` is observed mean, `n` effective count, `C` platform prior mean and `m` prior strength.

## 25. Audience interpretation/perception

Audience should not vote on “what the Bible says.”

Future questions can measure perception:

- What do viewers think the film is saying?
- How do viewers classify its stance?
- Which relationship/message do viewers see as central?
- What would viewers do in a dilemma?

This is reception data, not doctrinal authority.

## 26. Vote-first, crowd-second

For dilemmas and interpretation questions:

1. user answers independently;
2. community distribution is revealed after submission.

This reduces social-proof contamination.

## 27. Polarization and Editorial–Audience Gap

Normalized entropy remains a candidate for categorical polarization.

For comparable dimensions, calculate editorial–audience differences but never average them into a compromise truth score.

## 28. Rating maturity

Suggested public states:

- `EARLY`;
- `EMERGING`;
- `ESTABLISHED`;
- `HIGH CONFIDENCE`.

## 29. Anti-manipulation principles

Behavior signals can include:

- account age;
- verification state;
- rating velocity;
- extreme monotony;
- correlated bursts;
- source/referrer spikes;
- release timing;
- repeated coordinated target patterns;
- automation indicators.

Critical fairness rule:

**Weight must never depend on agreement with editorial ideology or theology.**

## 30. Transparent anomaly handling

If suspicious activity occurs:

- preserve raw votes;
- reduce aggregate influence where justified;
- log moderation/aggregation events;
- expose a public note if intervention is material;
- avoid silently rewriting history.

## 31. User reputation

Future reputation measures contribution quality, not orthodoxy/agreement.

Signals can include:

- useful scene notes;
- accepted factual corrections;
- well-reasoned counterarguments;
- helpful reviews;
- citation quality;
- moderation reliability.

## 32. Calibration plan

Before publishing authoritative scales:

1. select 20–30 films spanning genres, family structures, youth portrayals, relationship models and moral complexity;
2. have multiple editors analyze independently;
3. compare disagreement;
4. refine definitions;
5. identify dimensions with poor inter-rater reliability;
6. convert weak numeric dimensions to category/prose where needed;
7. version the rubric;
8. publish methodology.

Calibration should now include test cases for:

- sympathetic antiheroes;
- satire;
- ambiguous endings;
- dysfunctional families that the film critiques;
- absent consequences without clear endorsement;
- teen-centered films;
- relationship narratives;
- films with strong themes but weak moral clarity;
- films with morally dark content but truthful final stance.

## 33. External systems audited

Useful lessons:

- IMDb: weighted average and unusual-voting handling.
- Letterboxd: sparse-rating shrinkage and pattern mitigation.
- Rotten Tomatoes: critic/audience separation and verification concepts.
- Steam: anomaly windows can be excluded while preserving reviews.
- Common Sense Media: messages, role models, adult responsibility and consequences are separable analytical concerns.
- Kids-In-Mind: category-specific content plus `MESSAGES` and `DISCUSSION TOPICS` adds useful context.
- Plugged In: positive, spiritual, romantic/sexual, violent, language, substance and other content layers can coexist under a biblical framework.
- Movieguide / Christian Answers: Christian moral-review territory already exists.

DeepMovieReview differentiates by structuring story, relationships, themes, teaching signals, film form, scene evidence, difficult decisions and biblical evaluation into one inspectable graph.
